# Product roadmap recommendation

**Prepared:** 2026-11-02<br>
**Audience context:** The repository describes tuition/coaching providers and K–12 institutions. India is the user-selected discovery geography; first buyer segment, local market, daily-user profile, and customer demand are not established.<br>
**Decision status:** Advisory recommendation only. No new feature scope, priority, implementation, pilot, deployment, or release is approved by this review.

## Executive recommendation

Treat the delivered shared sign-in and Owner/Institution/first-Admin onboarding as a **platform foundation**, not as evidence that an operational education ERP is ready. Do not reactivate all legacy workflows or pursue competitor feature parity yet. First make the current product story and onboarding journey coherent, select one target segment and local context through customer discovery, and identify the first recurring operational job. Only then recommend a small tenant-safe operational slice through the existing approval gates.

This preserves the existing feature-record decisions about shared multi-institution direction, separation of Platform Owner from Institution Admin, and LegalEntity grouping without making a new architecture decision. Current source still has no operational education models or business APIs, so it does not demonstrate institution-scoped operational isolation. The `/institution` page is an account/Institution overview. Repository source and merge status do not prove runtime deployment, migration state, release, or customer acceptance.

### Meaningful alternatives

- **Onboarding-only proposition:** Test whether reliable institution setup and first-Admin access is valuable as a standalone product outcome. There is no buyer, retention, or willingness-to-pay evidence for that positioning.
- **Coaching/tutoring first:** Could align with a focused education-business workflow, but current legacy code and vendor claims do not prove local demand. Validate scheduling/session, attendance, and billing needs with the selected operators.
- **K–12 first:** May involve distinct school/district processes, family access, reporting, and integrations. Select a state/city and target institution type before determining requirements; no jurisdictional or compliance assessment is made here.

## Proposed staged path and exit gates

1. **Discovery and readiness (next):** Product Strategist and UI/UX Designer validate the current onboarding path with target users; the Product Owner selects the India institution segment, relevant state/city, buyer, operational user, and job to solve. Resolve whether the landing page is intended to be a live prospect funnel; substantiate or revise unsupported claims and decide what demo controls should do. Exit only when evidence and uncertainty are recorded and the user explicitly approves scope/priority.
2. **Design and architecture (only after scope approval):** UI/UX Designer specifies the approved journey; Frontend Engineer reviews feasibility. Architecture Analyst and, if persistence/contracts/query access change, Data Contracts Engineer review the cross-layer design. The user explicitly approves design and architecture/data direction before implementation planning.
3. **Implementation planning and pilot gate:** Plan sequenced vertical slices and risk-based verification; establish deployment/migration, security, support, accessibility, monitoring, rollback, and data-handling prerequisites. Existing source does not establish operational tenant isolation, so no real institution operational data should be assumed safe based only on membership/auth onboarding.
4. **Controlled pilot and evidence review:** Only after explicit user authorization and accepted readiness gates, observe the selected workflow with a deliberately scoped cohort. Compare outcomes against baselines, record support burden and incidents, then reprioritize.
5. **Expansion:** Expand to adjacent workflows or geographies only after repeatable evidence for the selected segment, operational/support capacity, and applicable data-governance review.

Candidate measures (targets **TBD** until a baseline is collected): Owner setup completion; invitation claim/review completion and support issues; time from Institution creation to first Admin sign-in; completion/time for the selected operational task; task success by role; institution-level data-isolation test results for approved operational paths; and support burden per institution. Metrics are proposals, not established product outcomes.

## Decisions needed from the Product Owner

1. Confirm the first target segment (for example, tuition/coaching provider or a specified K–12 institution type) and relevant state/city within India.
2. Identify the economic buyer and the person who performs daily administration.
3. Decide whether the immediate product outcome to validate is the delivered onboarding journey, an operational workflow, or another job; do not infer this from repository absence or vendor feature lists.
4. Confirm whether the public landing page and demo funnel should represent a current offer; if so, approve which claims/actions are truthful and supported.
5. After discovery, approve, reject, or revise a specific scope and priority. Any implementation recommendation remains gated by approved design, architecture/data review, and implementation plan.

## India product-discovery note — 2026-11-02

**Status:** Bounded Product Strategist desk research completed for both K–12 schools and coaching providers in India. This note appends findings; it does not change any approved product scope, architecture, or release decision. See the [discovery record](feature-delivery/india-initial-user-problem-discovery.md) for methods, segment hypotheses, ranked candidates, interview/prototype prompts, detailed source limitations, and the full evidence register.

- **Onboarding:** The Owner → manual first-Admin invitation → claim → Owner review → separate sign-in flow is implemented in source; the Institution Admin view is partial. No customer/user evidence establishes whether it solves a real onboarding problem, creates friction, or is unrelated to the first operational job. Technical/browser acceptance reported in the existing onboarding record is not customer discovery.
- **Segment hypotheses:** For K–12, a private-school proprietor/trust/management or group operations/finance buyer and office/registrar, head teacher, or class teacher users are plausible; procurement and role ownership vary and remain unverified. For coaching, a centre proprietor/director or group operations/finance buyer and batch coordinator, tutor, admissions, or fee staff users are plausible. Neither set is validated.
- **Workflow hypotheses:** The provisional top K–12 candidate is daily class/section roster and attendance exception follow-up; for coaching, batch/session roster and attendance follow-up. The group model and user job differ, so this is not a recommendation to build one generic feature. Alternatives include admissions/roster setup, fee records where relevant, and timetable/tests/reporting. All rankings are qualitative discovery hypotheses, not a feature priority or committed roadmap.
- **Recommended next problem to validate (unapproved):** For a specifically defined school type and coaching-provider type, determine whether maintaining a reliable current teaching-group roster and recording/following up attendance is a consequential recurring job—and whether the present Owner/Admin access flow helps or delays the first useful task. Start with recent real examples; customer validation is still required. No external outreach or prototype was authorized/performed.
- **India evidence limits:** MoSPI's 2025 household survey reports 27% of students taking/having taken private coaching (urban 30.7%, rural 25.5%); this is not a count of coaching businesses or evidence of provider software demand. The latest published UDISE+ source reviewed (2025–26, released 2026-08-17) provides national school-system context, not market size or a feature requirement. ASER 2024 is rural household research, not provider-workflow evidence. The Ministry's 2024 coaching guidelines and 2026 response show state/UT variation; this is not legal advice or an applicability finding. EduOps and CoachPlus pages are first-party vendor claims, not independently verified adoption or demand evidence.
- **Evidence boundary and next gate:** No interviews, observed use, telemetry, pilot outcomes, local buyer validation, pricing research, or willingness-to-pay evidence was collected. No feature scope is decision-ready. The immediate decision is whether to authorize further customer discovery around the proposed problem; a specific product scope/priority decision follows only after evidence and uncertainty are reviewed (or the user explicitly accepts the evidence limitation). UI/UX design, architecture/data design, implementation planning, and application-code changes remain blocked until then.
